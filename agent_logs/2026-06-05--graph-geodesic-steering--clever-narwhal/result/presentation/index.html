A field report for the architect of causalab

The geography inside Gemma

I built a task on your framework — 30 European countries, 8 compass directions, one question: “which country lies to the {direction} of {country}?” — and used the causalab pipeline, end to end, to ask whether the model's representation of those countries is literally a map. It is: two principal components of the country centroids regress onto latitude and longitude with R² 0.89 and 0.83, and a thin-plate-spline manifold charted by capital coordinates lets you walk between countries — with the model decoding every intermediate stop as a real place along the route.

EXHIBIT 01THE TASK

A causal model whose hidden variable has coordinates

tasks/country_borders was designed so that the framework's geometry machinery has something real to bite on: the intervention variable — the question's subject country — carries a metric embedding, country ↦ (lat, lon) of its capital. That single design choice is what makes manifold fitting, intrinsic-coordinate geodesics, and “does the midpoint decode to the country in between?” well-posed questions rather than metaphors.

The causal model. Inputs (country, direction, template) compose the prompt; the answer mechanism is a table lookup, raw_output = NEIGHBOR_OF[(country, direction)]. Note what this implies for every readout downstream: the intervened variable is the subject of the question — the correct observable is its neighbor, never the subject's own token.

EXHIBIT 02THE MAP IN THE WEIGHTS

Two principal components are latitude and longitude

Group the PCA-32 activations by answer country, average into 30 centroids, and regress each principal component on capital (lat, lon): PC0 R² = 0.893, PC1 R² = 0.830, PC2 R² = 0.042. The geographic plane isn't buried somewhere in the 2560-dimensional residual stream — it is the top of the spectrum, and it is two-dimensional, exactly as a map should be.

Worth pausing on what kind of claim this is: nothing in the task's training signal hands the model coordinates. The (lat, lon) embedding lives only in the causal model, as the parameterization your spline machinery consumes. The R² says the representation independently agrees with that parameterization — which is exactly the precondition for the steering experiments that follow.

EXHIBIT 03READING & PLACING THE VARIABLE

Score the consequence, patch where the model still computes

Two methodological decisions carry every result on this page, and both fall straight out of the causal model. First, the readout: intervening on the subject must be scored by neighbor mass — p(NEIGHBOR_OF[(patched_country, prompt_direction)]) — because the subject's own token is never the answer. Second, the site: a patch only demonstrates a working variable if downstream computation still consumes it.

Locate scan (centroid interchange, string-match) over layer × token position. Two loci appear: answer formation at late last_token, and the subject-entity representation along the country-token column. Both extremes are trivial in their own way — L33 writes the answer's marginal directly (the final layer leaves no computation to re-read the prompt's direction), L0 merely rewrites the token embedding. The mid-depth bump at L12/country is the interesting cell.
Site validation, direction-aware (argmax on the expected-neighbor set, 64 prompts). Russia is the diagnostic case — 8 neighbors spread over directions. A frozen final-layer marginal caps at 0.083; at L12/country the model re-binds the patched subject to the prompt's direction: 0.417, ten times the no-patch floor. All geometry work happens at this site (subspace pca_k32_ctok).
EXHIBIT 04THE TRAVERSAL

Four ways to cross Europe in activation space

A 2-D thin-plate-spline manifold is fit over the 30 L12 centroids, charted by capital (lat, lon) — your activation_manifold + path_steering stack, pointed at the new site. Between two endpoint countries we walk four kinds of path and, at every step, decode the patched activation back to a subject via its neighbor signature. The decoded itinerary — not the answer tokens — is the route as the model understood it.

The geodesic walks the corridor — unsupplied

Given only two endpoints, the manifold geodesic decodes a contiguous, geographically ordered chain on all four pairs — including the Mediterranean crossing Portugal→Greece, where the great-circle path goes by sea (Spain · France · Italy · Greece) rather than the land detour. Intermediate points of the chart decode to working representations of intermediate countries.

F2

Raw space replays a supplied route exactly

The oracle mode (piecewise-linear through true-border centroids) recovers every control point in order, through the 8-edge Portugal→Greece route. Steering and decode fidelity are not the bottleneck.

F3

The chord jumps; the data-driven graph routes through nonsense

The straight line always skips the middle in one hop (Spain→Ukraine; Bulgaria→Denmark). And the k-NN graph over PCA centroids has border-edge precision 0.27: it routes Greece→Norway through Iberia. Next exhibit.

EXHIBIT 05WHAT DISTANCES DON'T KNOW

Chart-alignable, not distance-recoverable

The sharpest lesson of the project is a dissociation. The representation supports a smooth geographic chart — geodesics decode as journeys, the PC plane regresses onto (lat, lon) — yet its raw metric does not encode the border graph. Concept geometry can be chart-alignable without being distance-recoverable, and the two controls below pin each side of that claim.

EXHIBIT 06WHAT THIS ADDS TO CAUSALAB

New pieces, and what they taught me about yours

I

tasks/country_borders

A complete task with a metrically embedded intervention variable: causal model + neighbor table + capital coordinates + templates + checker, with input_filter excluding unanswerable cells. It is, as far as I know, the first task in the repo whose hidden variable has a ground-truth 2-D geometry — built specifically to exercise the manifold/path-steering stack.

II

graph_path_steering (session analysis) + centroid_graph_geodesic (method)

Multi-mode path steering — manifold geodesic, raw straight chord, data-driven k-NN Dijkstra route, and a supplied oracle route — with a subject-signature decoder: each step's answer distribution is decoded to the subject whose direction-conditioned neighbor set (plus its own echo token) best explains it. It also reports k-NN-graph-vs-ground-truth edge quality as a first-class artifact.

III

patch_parity (session analysis)

A mechanism-parity diagnostic: the same value patched into the same prompts through interchange-with-source-representations, the interpolation executor, and a no-patch floor, with shape probing. It settled in one GPU-hour what could have been weeks of doubt — the two executors are bit-identical — and I'd argue for something like it living permanently in the framework as a sanity harness.

IV

Lesson: derive the readout from the causal model, mechanically

The intervened variable's own token is not, in general, the observable — here the correct observable is raw_output of the patched trace. Early experiments scored p(subject token) and read perfectly working interventions as chance for days. A framework-level guard — readouts constructed from the task's raw_output mechanism rather than from the intervened value — would make this entire class of error unrepresentable.

V

Lesson: causal scores need a computation budget

The locate heatmap's brightest cell (0.41 at L33/last_token) is the least informative one: at the final layer a full-residual patch just writes the answer's marginal. The same is true in reverse at L0 (token-embedding substitution). Annotating locate outputs with “layers of computation remaining at this site” would steer users to cells like L12/country, where the 0.22 score reflects a variable the model actually consumes.

VI

Lesson: metric semantics are a footgun

intervention_metric: string_match logs “mean score = 0.0333” (argmax accuracy, chance = 1/30) and kl logs “mean score = −0.05” (negated divergence, 0 is best) through the same log line. The manifold fit's reconstruction test printed chance-level accuracy at fit time and nobody noticed for a week. Tagging the scale onto the log message is a one-line fix with outsized value.

Stated limits. One model (Gemma3-4B-PT), one validated site, one seed, 16 prompts per path step. The (lat, lon) chart is supplied to the spline — the claim is alignment (R² 0.89/0.83, contiguity 0.78 vs 0.38 shuffled), not unsupervised discovery; indeed the k-NN result shows discovery from raw distances fails. One shuffle permutation so far; a null distribution over seeds is the obvious hardening, followed by a per-class battery over all 30 centroids (Norway's signature is dominated by Sweden/Finland and never decodes as itself) and replication at a second model scale.
