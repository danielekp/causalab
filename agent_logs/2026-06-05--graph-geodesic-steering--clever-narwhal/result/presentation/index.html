Walking the map inside the model

A steering pipeline that read as completely broken — every landscape at the 1/30 chance floor — turned out to be working perfectly. The probe was asking the wrong question. Once the readout was fixed and the site moved off the final layer, paths through activation space began decoding as journeys across Europe: contiguous chains of countries, in geographic order, deep enough in the network that the model still computes with every step.

EXHIBIT 01THE ANOMALY

Same patch, same site, two verdicts

The task: “Q: Which country lies to the {direction} of {country}? A:”. A locate scan said patching a country centroid at L33/last_token flips the model's answer with score 0.41. The steering executor, patching the same centroid at the same site, produced landscapes flat at 0.033 — exactly 1/30. One mechanism cannot be both causal and inert.

Locate scan, intervention score by (layer × token position). Two loci: answer formation at late last_token (peaking 0.41 at L33 — the final layer), and the subject-entity representation at the country token, early-to-mid depth. The 0.41 cell is bait.

Two confounded hypotheses were on the table:

H-bug — the steering executor silently drops the patch (a suspected (B,1,H) vs (B,H) rank mismatch in its replace_fn).

H-readout — both executors work and the flat landscapes are an artifact of how success is measured.

A four-arm A/B (patch_parity) was built to separate them: locate's mechanism, a verbatim replica of the steering executor, a shape-fixed variant, and a no-patch floor — same centroids, same prompts, same readout.

EXHIBIT 03THE TWIST

The variable is the question, not the answer

The causal model is raw_output = NEIGHBOR_OF[(country, direction)] — country is the question's subject, and the correct answer is one of its neighbors. Patch Spain into the residual stream and a working model should answer Portugal or France — never “Spain”. Every landscape, the reconstruction test, and the path-steering scorer were watching a token that is wrong by construction.

This single reading resolves the whole anomaly: locate's string-match scored against the counterfactual's expected output — the neighbor — so it saw the effect (0.41). The steering stack scored p(patched-country token) — never the right answer — so it saw the floor. Same mechanism, same effect, two readouts. The corrected metric is the neighbor-signature: mass on NEIGHBOR_OF[(steered_subject, prompt_direction)], per prompt.

EXHIBIT 04THE SITE

The final layer can't compute — L12 can

L33 is the last decoder layer: a full-residual patch at last_token fully determines the logits. The output freezes to the centroid's direction-marginal — there is no computation left to re-read the prompt's direction. The right home for the subject variable is mid-depth, at the country token itself, with ~20 layers of attention still downstream.

Direction-aware success (argmax on the expected-neighbor set), 64 prompts. Russia is the decisive case: 8 neighbors spread across directions — a frozen marginal can rarely pick the right one (0.083 at L33). At L12/country the model re-integrates the prompt's direction with the patched subject: 0.417, 10× floor.

EXHIBIT 05THE TRAVERSAL

Four ways to cross Europe in activation space

A 2-D thin-plate-spline manifold is fit over the 30 L12 country centroids, charted by capital (lat, lon). Between two endpoint countries we walk four paths and, at every step, decode the patched activation back to a subject via its neighbor-signature (echo-augmented). The decoded itinerary — not the answer tokens — is the route as the model understood it.

The straight line always skips the middle in one hop (Spain→Ukraine; Bulgaria→Denmark). The shuffled chains contain jumps no geography permits — Greece→Denmark, Spain→Poland — while canonical breaks are benign (Italy→Greece is the sea). What the shuffle removes is exactly the chart-alignment; what remains is the representation's own neighbor structure. The geodesic result is a property of the model's geometry, not of the spline.

EXHIBIT 07THE MEANING

What this changes, and what it doesn't

I

Readout semantics is a first-class failure mode

A fully correct intervention pipeline read as totally broken for an entire investigation because the metric scored the intervened variable where the task emits its consequence. Before debugging executors, derive the expected observable from the causal model — here, one line: raw_output = NEIGHBOR_OF[(country, direction)].

II

“It steers” is site-relative

The same centroid patch is trivial at L33 (writes the answer's marginal; direction unrecoverable), lexical at L0 (rewrites the prompt token), and computational at L12 — where downstream attention re-binds the patched subject to the prompt's direction. Causal scores without a computation budget at the site are not comparable.

III

Geometry lives in charts, not distances

The representation supports a smooth 2-D geographic chart — geodesics decode as journeys — yet its raw metric does not encode the border graph (precision 0.27). Concept geometry can be chart-alignable without being distance-recoverable: a caution for k-NN-based structure discovery, and a use-case for parameterized manifolds over unsupervised graphs.

IV

Interpolation between concepts is causally meaningful

Points between training concepts on the manifold are not garbage: they decode to other concepts' working representations, ordered by the chart. The 30 countries behave like samples from a continuous geographic surface the model can compute with anywhere along it.

Stated limits. One model (Gemma3-4B-PT), one site, one seed, 16 prompts per step; the lat/lon chart is supplied to the spline (the claim is alignment, not unsupervised discovery — the shuffle control separates exactly this); TPS at ambient dim 32 warns on conditioning; Norway never decodes as itself (its signature is dominated by Sweden/Finland) and mutual-neighbor endpoints flicker (Portugal↔Spain). One shuffle seed so far; a null distribution over permutations is the obvious hardening.
